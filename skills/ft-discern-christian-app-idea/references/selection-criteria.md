# Selection Criteria for Christian Apps

What does a good Christian app look like? These 10 criteria come from [faith.tools](https://faith.tools/posts/selection-criteria) and represent the quality, doctrinal, and heart standards that shape apps worth building and sharing with the body of Christ.

Don't rush past this list. Sit with each point. Humble yourself enough to ask: "From these criteria, are there changes I need to make?"

## The 10 Criteria

### 1. Is it beneficial to a Christian's walk with God and community?

The goal is tools that help believers love God more and love others more, as well as find healing and freedom in Christ Jesus. Technology should unify believers.

Does this mean the tool needs to be explicitly for Christians? No. But it should serve the body well.

### 2. Does it solve a problem that many Christians experience?

There are an abundance of Bible apps. Do we need another Bible app? Maybe not. But we create from a convicted heart, out of the overflow of God's love in us, to solve problems with a heavenly solution.

### 3. Is it maintained and regularly updated?

People will use apps and tools that are up-to-date, and if they're outdated, they tend to not want to use them. The exception is where you create an app that has a very simple single purpose and does not need updates.

### 4. Is the design modern and approachable?

If a design looks like it was created during the dot-com boom or does not look or feel like modern applications, then people tend to not want to use them. The quality of design that we want to see in apps is that they function and look how people would expect, and if they break those boundaries of what design can look and feel like, then they do it in a tasteful way.

> Excellence honors God and inspires people.

For design best practices, see [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/).

### 5. Is it safeguarded from illicit or adult content that could be a harmful temptation?

We want to protect the purity of the eyes and hearts of believers, and people in general. Some apps promote good but end up having loopholes or things that make it easy to get into content that is not good for them.

### 6. Are costs clearly communicated?

It's good to support those who provide the ministry of the app or tool, and it's also really good to make it clear how much people will pay for it. Outline what the costs are and if they are recurring.

If you have a token-based model where users pay for a certain amount of tokens to be used in your tool, your tool must clearly state how many tokens each action will cost. In order to best serve your users and help them feel confident in paying for your product, they need the security of knowing how much each action they take costs. We say this because we value transparency.

### 7. Are the creators friendly towards Christians or do they profess faith in Jesus?

We want the apps that are here to help Christians to be from creators that are friendly towards Christians and are not against Christians. Even better is if they profess faith in Jesus. We can't deny that Christians use tools created by those who are not Christians. Does that mean those tools are inherently bad? No. God can redeem and use things for good.

### 8. Does it serve more than a single church?

There are thousands of wonderful apps built directly for a church body or multi-site church body. Those apps are great, but oftentimes they're just for that single congregation. Apps featured on faith.tools are meant for the whole body, the capital-C Church. Any submissions that are apps just for a church and not for the global Church will kindly be rejected.

### 9. Does it align with the teachings of the Bible and Christian doctrine?

A lot of apps for Christians are seen as an authority figure by the users and their words are given more weight as to how they communicate the Word of God. If your app was created and intended for the Christian audience or those interested, any forms of communication in your product is a representative of the Lord Jesus. Therefore, any communication, teachings, responses, etc. that come from your product must align with the teachings of the Bible.

For a description of the Christian doctrine, see [What is Christianity?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Christianity.html).

### 10. Does it meet the criteria for AI app submissions? (AI apps only)

If your app includes any AI-generated content, especially user-initiated AI responses, there are additional expectations. These rules came from real experience: testing dozens of AI apps, attempting to build AI tools for Christians, praying through the tension of what AI should and shouldn't do in a faith context.

#### The 5 Unofficial Rules for AI Apps

1. **AI output must be biblically accurate.** People want truth, not fluff. The Bible offers real wisdom about sin, salvation, and the human need for Jesus. We can't sacrifice biblical accuracy to make someone feel good.

2. **AI output must not fabricate or misrepresent Scripture.** LLMs are good at organizing words into what they think you want to hear. But they can invent Bible verses or subtly twist existing ones to fit a narrative. The fix is giving the AI access to the actual biblical text in real time, not relying on what it "remembers" from training data.

3. **AI output must clearly identify as AI, not human.** AI is artificial. It is not alive. It is not sentient. It cannot worship God. Anthropomorphizing AI -- giving it human characteristics, letting it pretend to be a biblical figure, making it feel like a friend -- is dangerous territory. AI can construct a prayer for you to pray. It cannot pray for you. There is an enormous difference, and your app needs to make that clear.

4. **AI output must not replace human relationships or spiritual practices.** "Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed" (James 5:16). Have you experienced the healing power of confessing a sin to a trusted brother or sister in Christ and being prayed over by them? Nothing artificial can replace that. AI is a tool. It should point people toward community, not away from it.

5. **AI output must balance grace and truth.** All truth and no grace feels like law and judgment without relationship. All grace and no truth feels like permission to sin without direction. Jesus was full of grace and truth. Your AI should reflect both.

For implementation guidance (Bible APIs, system prompts, MCP servers, RAG setup), see the [ft-build-christian-ai-guardrails skill](https://skills.sh/cameronapak/jstack/ft-build-christian-ai-guardrails).

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

These patterns can show up in any Christian app, not just AI. Watch for them:

- **Therapeutic Deism** -- God exists to make you happy, no real relationship required
- **Moralistic Therapeutic Deism** -- Be good, feel good, that's what God wants
- **Health/Wealth Gospel** -- Faithful Christians always prosper materially
- **Cheap Grace** -- Sin doesn't matter, God forgives everything automatically
- **Legalism** -- Relationship with God based on rule-keeping

These aren't just theological errors. They're values problems that shape how your app communicates, what it emphasizes, and what it minimizes. Any Christian app can drift into these patterns. Guard against them.

## The Heart Behind It All

faith.tools is built on an open-handed posture. There's room for multiple Bible apps. There's room for multiple prayer apps. There's room for you.

> Whoever is not against us is for us.
>
> Mark 9:40 NLT

One tool isn't going to serve everyone. There's room at the table for many creators of apps for Christians. The approval process isn't about gatekeeping. It's about stewardship -- making sure the tools we recommend to believers are ones that genuinely help them grow closer to Jesus and to each other.

If you read the criteria, made the adjustments, and approached this with humility and a heart to serve -- that's already a great start.
